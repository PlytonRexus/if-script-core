## PROLOGUE: WHAT HARRY POTTER KNOWS

Harry James Potter is eleven years old and believes, provisionally, in four things:

1. The universe operates according to consistent laws.
2. Consistent laws can be discovered by careful observation.
3. Human beings are spectacularly bad at careful observation.
4. He is probably not an exception to rule number three, which is why rule number two requires writing things down.

His stepfather, Professor Michael Verres-Evans, is a biochemist at Oxford. His mother, Lily Potter née Evans, died when he was a baby — killed, everyone says now, by a dark wizard named Voldemort who was himself destroyed in the attempt. Harry grew up surrounded by textbooks, peer-reviewed journals, and a father who considered "because that's how it works" to be a moral failure of an explanation.

When the Hogwarts letter arrived, Harry spent three days constructing alternative hypotheses for how it could have been faked before his stepfather took him to Diagon Alley and Harry held a wand and the air around him turned faintly silver and smelled of ozone, and Harry was forced to update significantly on the hypothesis that magic was real.

He has since spent two weeks reading every magical text he could obtain, filling four notebooks with observations, and forming seventeen distinct hypotheses about the nature of magical causality, none of which he can yet test. He is, for the first time in his life, confronted with a domain where he lacks the conceptual tools to even frame the right questions.

He finds this absolutely intoxicating.

## ACT ONE: THE TRAIN

---

### [N01] — COMPARTMENT OF STRANGERS

The Hogwarts Express is a steam locomotive that runs on a track between London and Scotland, which is itself mildly interesting since the thermodynamics should be wrong. Harry has already noted this in his notebook (Observation #47: *The train appears to operate at efficiency values inconsistent with coal combustion. Possible explanations: (a) enchanted fuel, (b) glamour over non-coal fuel source, (c) the engine is not actually doing the thermodynamic work and is purely aesthetic. Require more data.*).

He is sitting alone in a compartment, writing furiously, when the door opens. Two people enter: a girl with a bushy brown mane of hair who is clutching a very large book and appears to have been crying recently, and a pale blond boy whose posture radiates the specific confidence of someone who has been told, very often, that his posture should radiate confidence.

The girl: "Sorry, is this compartment taken? All the others are—"

The blond boy, who came in behind her and clearly did not intend to share with her: "I require the use of this compartment."

Harry looks at them both. The girl is reading *Advanced Charm Theory: A Structural Analysis* — which Harry has also read, and found frustrating because it describes what charms do without explaining *why* charms do it. The blond boy is wearing robes that cost approximately as much as Harry's entire wardrobe.

**What does Harry do?**

**→ CHOICE 1A:** Invite them both in and immediately start interrogating them about their magical backgrounds. Harry is curious and compartment politics seem less interesting than data collection. **[GO TO N02-A]**

**→ CHOICE 1B:** Invite only the girl in — she's reading a theory text, which means she might be able to discuss actual ideas. The blond boy's posture suggests he'll want to talk about his family. **[GO TO N02-B]**

**→ CHOICE 1C:** Close his notebook, introduce himself neutrally to both, and say nothing about his background until he has a better model of what's happening here. Information asymmetry is an asset. **[GO TO N02-C]**

---

### [N02-A] — THE OPEN INVITATION

"Come in, both of you," Harry says. "I'm Harry Potter. I'm making observations."

The blond boy's expression goes through several rapid changes. The name *Harry Potter* triggers something — recognition, recalibration, the quick internal accounting of social status. He sits down across from Harry with a practiced ease. "Draco Malfoy. My father—"

"Tell me about magic," Harry says. "How did it feel the first time you performed a spell? Not the spell itself, just the phenomenology. The internal experience."

Draco stares. No one has ever asked him this. "I — it felt like exhaling."

The girl sits beside Harry. "Hermione Granger. For me it felt more like... completing a circuit? I've thought about it quite a lot." She sounds slightly defensive about the amount she's thought about it.

"That's fascinating," Harry says, and means it. "Because I've been theorizing about whether the mechanism is more akin to an information-processing model or an energy-transduction model, and phenomenological reports from experienced subjects could be genuinely useful." He pauses. "I grew up Muggle. I don't know any of the social rules yet. Tell me what I'm doing wrong."

There is a silence. Then Hermione starts laughing, surprised, and even Draco's mouth does something adjacent to a smile.

**→ GO TO N03-TRAIN**

---

### [N02-B] — THE SELECTIVE INVITATION

"Come in," Harry says to the girl. Then, to the blond boy: "Sorry. Full."

The blond boy's face does something complicated. "Do you know who I am?"

"Not yet," Harry says pleasantly. "But I'm Harry Potter, and I've found that's often relevant to people, so I'm mentioning it preemptively."

The blond boy leaves — he doesn't slam the door, which Harry notes as interesting. Controlled. He closes it with deliberate quiet.

The girl sits. "I'm Hermione Granger. That was Draco Malfoy. You've probably made an enemy."

"Or I've made a mystery," Harry says. "People who are controlled when they want to be angry are usually either disciplined or afraid. Both are interesting." He opens his notebook. "Can I ask you some questions about magic? You were reading *Advanced Charm Theory.* What did you make of the author's refusal to engage with the question of magical source?"

Hermione's eyes light up. She has never, she will realize later, been asked *what she made of* a book's argument by another eleven-year-old.

They talk for four hours. Harry fills six pages.

**→ GO TO N03-TRAIN**

---

### [N02-C] — THE NEUTRAL PLAY

"There's room," Harry says, neither warmly nor coldly. He closes his notebook with deliberate casualness that he hopes suggests the notebook is not important. "I'm Harry. I'm a first-year."

"Draco Malfoy." The blond boy sits, assessing. "And you're Muggle-born?"

"Half-blood," Harry says, because he has read enough to know this distinction matters here. "My mother was a witch. She died when I was a baby." He offers nothing further.

The girl sits beside him. "Hermione Granger. Muggle-born." She says it slightly defiantly, like she's expecting a challenge.

Draco's expression suggests he has filed away approximately three different judgments. Harry files away that expression.

They sit in something that isn't quite silence. Harry is doing what he does when he has inadequate data: watching, cataloguing, generating hypotheses. He notices: Draco looks at Harry's scar and then looks away quickly. Hermione looks at Draco's robes and then straightens her spine half an inch. Everyone in this compartment is performing something.

Harry decides to find out what.

"Tell me about your families," he says, "and I'll tell you about mine."

**→ GO TO N03-TRAIN**

### [N03-TRAIN] — WHAT THE PREFECT SAYS

Regardless of how the first meeting went, the journey continues.

A seventh-year prefect named Percy Weasley stops by to tell them about Houses, speaking with the authority of someone who considers institutional structures to be intrinsically meaningful. Harry listens carefully. Ravenclaw: cleverness and learning. Gryffindor: courage. Hufflepuff: loyalty and work. Slytherin: ambition and resourcefulness.

"These seem like descriptions rather than criteria," Harry says.

Percy blinks. "I'm sorry?"

"What is the *actual* mechanism by which a hat determines which category a person falls into? Is it behavioral prediction? Self-identification? Brain-state measurement? The criteria you've listed are all output-variables. I want to understand the input-variables."

Percy leaves somewhat earlier than he intended to.

Hermione (if present) looks at Harry with an expression that contains equal parts delight and mild alarm. Draco (if present) looks at Harry with an expression that suggests he is significantly revising some assumption he had made.

Later, as the train descends toward Scotland and the sky outside turns a spectacular shade of pre-storm purple, Harry stares out the window and thinks about what it means that there is a whole world hidden inside the one he grew up in, operating according to different rules, populated by people who have been marinating in those different rules for generations. He is going to be behind in the cultural knowledge. He is going to be ahead in the methodological knowledge. He needs allies.

The question is: which kind.

**What does Harry prioritize as the train ends?**

**→ CHOICE 3A:** Harry tries to solidify his friendship with Hermione (and Draco if present), treating the social dimension as genuinely important. **[GO TO N04-A]**

**→ CHOICE 3B:** Harry retreats into his notebook for the last hour, deciding that first-order relationships matter less than second-order information. He eavesdrops carefully. **[GO TO N04-B]**

---

### [N04-A] — THE SOCIAL INVESTMENT

Harry spends the last hour of the train ride being deliberately, effortfully friendly. He asks questions that aren't about magic — about what Draco thinks is funny (political satire, he says, which Harry believes), about what Hermione is most afraid of failing at (everything, she says, which Harry also believes). He volunteers, in return, that he is most afraid of being wrong about something important while being confident he is right. That he has been wrong before in ways that mattered and that the feeling of retroactive recognition is one of the worst feelings he knows.

This surprises both of them. Children from confident families don't usually talk about being wrong.

By the time the train reaches Hogsmeade station, Harry has rough internal models of two people. They are imprecise models. They will need updating. But they are a start.

**→ GO TO N05-SORTING**

### [N04-B] — THE OBSERVATION

Harry sits with his notebook open on his knee but doesn't write in it visibly. He listens to the compartments around him through the thin walls — fragments of conversation about Quidditch, about families, about houses, about a boy who apparently turned his teacher's wig blue in a fit of accidental magic last term. He notes which stories people are telling each other about what Hogwarts is. He is mapping the mythology before he arrives at the territory.

He hears, from the compartment behind him, a conversation between two older students about something called *the Restricted Section* of the library and why certain books are kept there. He writes this down. He hears someone mention that the Defense Against the Dark Arts position is cursed — no teacher has lasted more than a year. He writes this down too, with a small star next to it.

## ACT TWO: THE CASTLE

---

### [N05-SORTING] — THE HAT

The Great Hall is large enough that Harry spends the first thirty seconds estimating whether the ceiling is enchanted or structural and concludes it is almost certainly a portal to actual sky (too responsive to wind patterns; a painting would not ripple). He is the last student called to be sorted.

When the Sorting Hat is placed on his head, it does not wait.

*Harry Potter,* it says inside his skull, and its voice is neither male nor female, just ancient and very dry. *My, my. You've been thinking about this, haven't you.*

"About what?"

*About what I'm going to say. You've constructed approximately six models of this interaction and you're prepared to game three of them.*

Harry experiences a moment of intense discomfort. "Are you Legilimency-capable?"

*I am the accumulated wisdom of a thousand years of human classification. I am less interested in reading your mind than in pointing out that you are trying to read mine.* A pause. *You know which house would serve your goals. You've worked it out. Ravenclaw for the library access and the collaborative intellectual environment. Slytherin for the political power. Gryffindor for the symbolic capital. Hufflepuff,* another pause, *you haven't really considered.*

"Hufflepuff's sorting criteria seem to select for qualities I don't think I have."

*You're wrong about that, but we'll leave it for now.* The Hat seems to settle into a different mode. *Here is the real question, Mr. Potter. What do you want, actually, underneath all the methodology?*

Harry thinks. He doesn't try to hide the answer because he suspects the Hat can already see it.

"I want to understand magic well enough to know what it actually *is*. And I want to — I want to make sure that whatever happened to my mother doesn't happen to other people."

*Revenge or prevention?*

"Prevention. Revenge requires an enemy. I don't have a fixed enemy. I have a problem."

The Hat is silent for a moment. Then: *Do you know what makes a person dangerous, Mr. Potter? Not cruelty. Not strength. The willingness to follow an idea all the way to its conclusion regardless of what they find there. You have that quality. Used well, it is extraordinary. Used poorly—*

"I know," Harry says. "I know. Where do I go?"

**What does Harry add?**

**→ CHOICE 5A:** "Put me where I'll have the best access to information and the most intellectual peers." **[GO TO N06-RAVENCLAW]**

**→ CHOICE 5B:** "Put me where I'll have the most influence over the shape of things." **[GO TO N06-SLYTHERIN]**

**→ CHOICE 5C:** "I don't want to choose. Put me where I belong, not where I want to be." **[GO TO N06-GRYFFINDOR]**

### [N06-RAVENCLAW] — THE EAGLE'S TOWER

"RAVENCLAW!" the Hat shouts.

The table of blue-and-bronze students applauds with the slightly self-conscious enthusiasm of people who have been told they are intelligent so often they have become mildly anxious about not seeming intelligent enough. Harry walks over and sits between a second-year who is reading a book under the table and a first-year girl with dark braids who introduces herself as Padma Patil.

That night, in Ravenclaw Tower, the prefect explains that the entrance requires answering a riddle rather than speaking a password. Harry asks how this interacts with extreme edge cases — what if the riddle is unanswerable? The prefect says there is always an answer. Harry asks how they know. The prefect says they have never encountered a case where there wasn't. Harry notes this is not the same thing, but lets it go.

His dormitory is shared with three other boys: Anthony Goldstein, who has already organized his bookshelf by subject and then by author within subject; Michael Corner, who has an elaborate constellation map pinned above his bed; and Terry Boot, who asks Harry approximately forty questions about what it is like to grow up Muggle and then falls asleep mid-sentence.

Harry lies awake for two hours. He is in the tower of the clever. He is looking at the stars through a window slit and thinking about the fact that his mother died because of something she knew, or something she did, or something she was, and that he does not yet know enough to know which of those it was.

He opens his notebook.

*Hypothesis 1,* he writes. *Voldemort is not actually dead. The available evidence — a one-year-old survives a killing curse; a self-proclaimed immortal vanishes without a body being found — does not support the conclusion that death occurred. Counterarguments: …*

He writes for an hour. When he finally sleeps, he dreams of silver light.

**→ GO TO N07-FIRST-WEEKS**

### [N06-SLYTHERIN] — THE DUNGEON

"SLYTHERIN!" the Hat shouts.

There is a complicated sound from the Slytherin table — not entirely enthusiastic, because Harry Potter is not the right sort of Slytherin in the minds of people who care about the right sort, but also not cold, because Harry Potter is Harry Potter and social currency is social currency.

Draco Malfoy, seated at the table already, looks at Harry with an expression that contains approximately seventeen things at once, most of which Harry cannot yet decode.

That night in the dungeons, Harry learns: Slytherin common room is underground and the ceiling is lake-bottom glass. Green light filters through. It is, objectively, stunning. He notes this. He also notes that the aesthetic choices of a House probably correlate with how it selects and socializes its members, and that living under the lake, below everyone else, visible to the creatures above but not to other people, probably does something to how Slytherins think about surveillance and concealment.

His dormitory-mates are Draco, Crabbe, Goyle, Blaise Zabini, and Theodore Nott. Crabbe and Goyle do not read. Zabini is watchful in the specific way of someone who was taught to be watchful before he could read. Nott sits alone and looks at the dark water above and does not say anything to anyone.

Draco takes Harry aside after the others are asleep. "Why Slytherin?" he asks. He is not being aggressive. He is genuinely curious.

"I wanted to understand power," Harry says, which is true and not the whole truth. "Why did you want me sorted here?"

Draco blinks. "What makes you think I—"

"You wanted it. I could tell from the moment the Hat said the word. Why?"

A long pause. Outside the dark glass, something enormous and tentacled drifts past. Then Draco says: "Because I think you're going to matter. And I'd rather be on the right side of that."

Harry considers this. It is the most honest thing Draco has said to him. He files it carefully.

**→ GO TO N07-FIRST-WEEKS**

### [N06-GRYFFINDOR] — THE TOWER

"GRYFFINDOR!" the Hat shouts.

The red-and-gold table erupts. The Weasley twins begin some kind of chant. Harry walks over in a slight daze, not because of the noise but because the Hat said, before it announced his house: *Bravery isn't the absence of fear, Mr. Potter. It is the willingness to do what needs doing even when your models tell you the cost is high. That is what you are, underneath everything else. Remember that.*

He sits next to Ron Weasley, who has red hair and a smear of something on his nose and who says, immediately: "That was dead long, wasn't it? The Hat barely touched most people. What did it say to you?"

"It asked me questions," Harry says.

"What kind of questions?"

"Philosophical ones." Harry looks at Ron. "Are you a Weasley? I heard people on the train say the Weasleys are always Gryffindors."

"Yeah. All seven of us, going back hundreds of years." Ron says this with the mix of pride and mild exhaustion of someone who has inherited something very heavy. "I've got five brothers ahead of me here."

"That must make it difficult to be distinctively yourself," Harry says thoughtfully.

Ron stares at him. "You're a bit strange, aren't you?"

"Almost certainly," Harry agrees. "But I find that people who say things like that usually mean they haven't decided yet whether strange is good or bad."

Ron looks at him for a moment. Then he grins. "Fair enough."

**→ GO TO N07-FIRST-WEEKS**

### [N07-FIRST-WEEKS] — THE ANOMALY

The first month at Hogwarts produces the following confirmed facts, added to Harry's notebook:

*Magic responds to intent, not mechanism.* The same wand motion produces different effects depending on the caster's mental state. This is deeply strange from a physics standpoint and implies that either (a) magic is information-theoretic at a fundamental level, (b) consciousness has causal power over magical fields, or (c) Harry's models of physics need to be substantially revised.

*Memory and magic interact.* He observes, in Charms class, that students who grew up with magic perform certain spells more easily despite no practice advantage — as though the exposure itself has primed something. He calls this the *ambient induction effect* in his notes.

*Professor Quirrell is interesting.* The Defense Against the Dark Arts professor is allegedly something of a joke — a nervous, stuttering man who spent a year traveling and came back changed. But Harry notices: Quirrell's stutter disappears when he is thinking hard about something. Quirrell's nervous tics are perfectly consistent. They never vary. Harry has a rule: behavior that never varies is either genuine or performed, and genuine nervous behavior varies.

*The Restricted Section exists for reasons that are not about student safety.* A third-year tells him the books there are restricted because they're "dangerous." But when pressed on what kind of dangerous, the answer is always vague. Books are dangerous because of what they cause people to do, not what they do themselves. Which means the question is: what would people *do* if they read those books?

One evening in the library, Harry finds something.

He has been researching the Killing Curse — not because he wants to cast it, but because the story of his own survival doesn't make physical sense and he needs to understand the mechanism before he can evaluate the "prophecy" dimension. He has found two relevant texts. In the margin of one of them, in very small, very neat handwriting that is clearly not his own, someone has written: *The curse does not kill. It disconnects. The question is: from what?*

Harry stares at this for a long time.

Then he turns the page and finds, in the same hand: *There is a countermeasure. It is not love. Love is a description, not a mechanism. Find the actual mechanism and you will find that it is something much simpler and much stranger than anyone has told you.*

**What does Harry do with this?**

**→ CHOICE 7A:** He carefully photographs the pages with a spell he has just invented (using his understanding of light and chemistry to create a magical daguerreotype), and says nothing to anyone. He is going to pursue this alone. **[GO TO N08-A]**

**→ CHOICE 7B:** He copies the text and immediately brings it to the person he trusts most — either Hermione (Ravenclaw/Gryffindor path) or Draco (Slytherin path). **[GO TO N08-B]**

**→ CHOICE 7C:** He copies the text and brings it directly to Professor Quirrell, who teaches defense, and whose handwriting he has not yet seen but intends to compare. **[GO TO N08-C]**

## ACT THREE: THE INVESTIGATION

---

### [N08-A] — THE SOLO PURSUIT

Harry works alone. This has advantages and disadvantages. The advantages: no one else's priors contaminate his analysis. The disadvantages: no one else's priors contaminate his analysis.

Over the next three weeks he constructs the following:

The Killing Curse, *Avada Kedavra*, appears linguistically to derive from Aramaic roots meaning "I will destroy as I speak" — a performative utterance theory of magic, which is interesting. It is classified as *Unforgivable*, which means there is institutional investment in keeping it terrifying and beyond analysis, which Harry has learned to treat as a signal worth investigating rather than a reason to stop. Forbidden things are forbidden because someone has an interest in forbidding them, and that someone's interests are information.

The magical theory of *soul* is treated in older texts as a technical term, not a metaphorical one. Several pre-Ministry texts discuss the soul as having measurable properties: it can be damaged (Dementors), separated from the body (certain rituals), and apparently, destroyed (the Killing Curse). None of these texts explain *what* the soul actually is at a functional level — they treat the term as self-explanatory, which is what people do with concepts they have inherited rather than derived.

The countermeasure note said: *Find the actual mechanism.*

Harry is working on it. He is also working on the handwriting problem. He obtains a sample of Quirrell's writing from a student who kept an annotated essay. He compares it to the library margin notes.

They are not a match.

So it wasn't Quirrell. Harry updates his hypothesis list. He adds: *The annotator is someone who has had access to this specific book, has done original research on the curse mechanism, and who wanted someone specific to find this note. Or who didn't care who found it. The latter seems more dangerous.*

One night, very late, he falls asleep in the library and dreams of a man with red eyes standing in front of a mirror, and when he wakes, his scar is burning.

**→ GO TO N09-QUIRRELL-ENCOUNTER**

### [N08-B] — THE ALLY

Harry brings the text to Hermione (in the Ravenclaw/Gryffindor path) or Draco (in the Slytherin path). The conversation is different in each case, but the result is the same: he now has a research partner with a different background and different priors, and the collision of those priors produces things he would not have reached alone.

**If Hermione:** She immediately pulls seven books from her memory of the library catalogue and is back within twenty minutes with three of them, one of which contains a footnote Harry missed. The footnote references a ninth-century text about *sacrifice-based protection*, which is not the same as love-based protection. The mechanism involves *willing death* — not grief, not love, but the specific act of choosing death without fighting it. "This is a magical contract theory," Hermione says quietly. "The person who dies for someone creates a binding. A ward." "So my mother created a ward," Harry says slowly. "By choosing to die rather than move." "By choosing to die *for you specifically*," Hermione says. She looks at him with an expression that is careful and kind. "Harry. You know what this means, don't you?" "It means Voldemort can't touch me *because* of the ward. But wards can be dissolved. There must be a counter-condition."

**If Draco:** He goes pale, then very still. Then he says something Harry does not expect: "My father talks about this. About the Dark Lord. He says — he says the Dark Lord left something behind. Not just the scar." He does not say what his father believes the Dark Lord left behind. Harry has to ask three times before Draco says, very quietly: "There's a theory. That he split his — that there are pieces of him, still existing, somewhere. That's why no one is sure he's actually gone." "Where does your father believe these pieces are?" Draco looks at Harry's forehead. He doesn't say anything. He doesn't need to.

**→ GO TO N09-QUIRRELL-ENCOUNTER**

### [N08-C] — THE DIRECT APPROACH

Harry brings the text to Quirrell.

This is, as it turns out, a decision that changes everything.

He knocks on the door of the Defense classroom after hours, notes folded in his pocket. Quirrell opens it, already wearing his turban, his expression simultaneously surprised and not-quite-surprised in a way Harry clocks immediately. He invites Harry in with a gesture.

"I f-found something in the library," Harry says. "I wanted your perspective, since you teach Defense." He does not mention that he suspects the notes may be connected to Quirrell. He passes over the text.

Quirrell reads it. He reads it for what seems like too long, given that it is only two sentences. Then he says, in a voice from which the stutter has entirely vanished: "Where, exactly, in the library?"

Harry has not been expecting the stutter to vanish. He has been preparing for this possibility, but preparing and encountering are different. He keeps his face neutral. "The second restricted shelf, filed under *Cursed Objects, Theoretical.* There was no borrowing record, so it's been there for at least forty years."

Quirrell hands the paper back. The stutter is back now. "F-fascinating. I'm not sure what to m-make of it. You should be c-careful, Mr. Potter, about drawing conclusions from anonymous marginal notes."

"Of course," Harry says. He folds the paper. "Can I ask you something unrelated, Professor?"

"Y-yes?"

"You went on a sabbatical before coming here. You traveled. Where did you go?"

A pause. "Many p-places."

"Did you visit Albania?"

The air in the room does something. It is not physical, but Harry's threat-detection system, which is currently operating on the same principles as a rabbit's, fires at full intensity.

"I don't see how that's r-relevant—"

"Forgive me," Harry says. "I'm curious by nature. I'll think about what you said. About anonymous sources." He smiles. He leaves.

Outside the door, he stands in the corridor for ten seconds and breathes.

He knows. He does not know exactly what he knows, but he knows it is large and dangerous and that he has just told it that he is asking questions.

This was possibly a mistake.

**→ GO TO N09-QUIRRELL-ENCOUNTER (ACCELERATED)**

### [N09-QUIRRELL-ENCOUNTER] — THE LESSON

*Note: If you arrived here from [N08-C], the following scene occurs the very next day. If you arrived from [N08-A] or [N08-B], it occurs approximately six weeks later, after Harry's investigation has produced more groundwork.*

Professor Quirrell announces, at the beginning of November, that he will be offering supplemental Defense lessons to any first-year who wishes to attend. The sessions will be held on Wednesday evenings. The topic: "the philosophy of defense — why we resist dark magic, what we are actually defending against."

Almost no one attends. It is a Wednesday. There is Quidditch practice. The common rooms are warm.

Harry attends.

He is the only first-year. He is, in fact, the only student. He sits in the third row, in the center, because that is the position that maximizes sight lines. Quirrell sits on the desk at the front, and his stutter, Harry notes, is very subdued tonight.

"Tell me, Mr. Potter," Quirrell says, "what do you think evil is?"

"A category error," Harry says immediately. "The label 'evil' is applied to actions that cause large amounts of suffering without adequate justification. But the label tells you nothing about mechanism or motive, which are the things you need to understand if you want to actually reduce the incidence of those actions."

Quirrell tilts his head. "You don't believe in evil as a real force."

"I believe that people do terrible things for reasons that made sense to them, and that the first step to stopping terrible things is understanding those reasons. The category 'evil' shortcuts that understanding."

"What if," Quirrell says, "the reasons, once understood, do not become more forgivable? What if understanding the mechanism simply gives you a clearer picture of something that deserves the word evil, after all?"

Harry is quiet for a moment. "Then I will use the word evil as a shorthand, while maintaining the full picture underneath. What I will never do is use it as a *terminal* explanation. Evil explains nothing. It just names the explanandum."

Quirrell looks at him with an expression Harry has never seen on an adult directed at him. It is something between respect and hunger.

"I was right about you," Quirrell says. The stutter is completely gone.

**What does Harry say?**

**→ CHOICE 9A:** "Right about what?" — Harry engages, presses for information, treats this as a chance to extract data. **[GO TO N10-A]**

**→ CHOICE 9B:** Harry says nothing. He recognizes the bait and stays silent, watching. **[GO TO N10-B]**

**→ CHOICE 9C:** "I've been right about you too." Harry reveals that he knows something — not everything, but enough to signal. **[GO TO N10-C]**

### [N10-A] — THE WILLING STUDENT

"Right about what?" Harry asks.

The conversation that follows lasts two hours and covers: the nature of magical dark arts, the history of the first Voldemort war, the theoretical possibility of magical immortality, and — this is where it gets precise and dangerous — the mechanics of what happens when a soul, for lack of a better word, is fragmented.

Quirrell speaks with the confidence of someone who has studied these things intimately. He does not tell Harry everything. But what he tells Harry is enough.

By the end of the lesson, Harry knows: Voldemort is not dead. There is something of him still existing. The object housing it is somewhere in Hogwarts. And Quirrell knows where.

Harry does not yet know that Quirrell is, in a meaningful sense, that object's host. He is three correct inferences away from this conclusion, and he needs more data.

He leaves the classroom with his notebook full and his mind running faster than he can write.

**→ GO TO N11-THE-DISCOVERY**

### [N11-THE-DISCOVERY] — WHAT IS BEING GUARDED

*Note: The timeline varies depending on your path, but all paths converge on the following information being established by December.*

Harry, through one route or another, has established:

### [N12-DUMBLEDORE] — THE MEETING

### [N13-C] — THE UNILATERAL DECISION

Walking back to his dormitory, Harry is thinking very fast. He needs to act before Quirrell does.

He needs, for possibly the first time in his life, to trust his own judgment on something genuinely high-stakes without a teacher's oversight.

### [N14-THE-PLAN] — THREE MONTHS, ONE NIGHT

The second problem with the plan: it assumes Harry can navigate the obstacles alone.

The third problem with the plan: it does not account for the possibility that Quirrell is already inside.

### [ENDING-A] — THE INSTRUMENT
*Reachable from: Slytherin path + [N13-C] (unilateral) + [N17-THE-TRUTH] + [N18-A-ii]*

Harry plays the long game. The conversation in the chamber was a delay tactic.

While Voldemort spoke, Harry used a charm he has been developing since October — a copying charm applied not to paper but to air itself, a kind of magical recording — to capture everything Voldemort said. He has the full confession, the full inventory of Horcruxes, the full theory, pressed into a small crystal in his left fist.

He gets out with the Stone and the recording.

He does not tell Dumbledore everything immediately. He tells him enough to deal with Quirrell. He keeps the recording.

Over the next six years, Harry systematically destroys the Horcruxes. He does not ask permission. He acquires the necessary knowledge, creates his own tools, and acts. By the time Voldemort is truly dead, Harry is seventeen, and he has done it largely alone, and the people who know what he is capable of look at him with something that is not quite fear and not quite awe.

He wins.

He has become, in the process, the kind of person who treats everyone as an instrument toward a goal, because that is what the goal required. He is aware of this. He has extensive notes on it.

He does not know if this is a tragedy or just a trade.

### [ENDING-C] — THE SACRIFICE

"You could have died."

"I calculated approximately twenty percent mortality risk," Harry says. "Given the alternative outcomes—"

"Harry." Dumbledore's voice is very quiet. "This is not a conversation about probability."

"Then what is it a conversation about?"

Dumbledore looks at him. "About whether you are all right."

Harry considers this. "I will be," he says finally. "I think." And then, because Dumbledore is one of approximately three people Harry thinks actually means the question: "Ask me again in a week."

*THE END. (He means it. Ask him again in a week. He will be honest.)*

### [ENDING-D] — THE TRANSFORMATION
*Reachable from: [N13-A] (full partnership with Dumbledore) + [N17-THE-TRUTH] + [N18-A-i]*

Harry is serious about the proposal.

It takes three years. It is the hardest work Harry has ever done, and he has done hard work.

Voldemort's fragment goes — somewhere. Not destroyed. Harry does not ask questions about where, because the answer will be complicated and he will not be able to pretend he doesn't know it, and some knowledge makes action obligatory. He has learned, this year, to be strategic about what he lets himself know.

The research Harry undertakes over the next three years, with Dumbledore's reluctant support and the annotated texts Voldemort's fragment provided (through a channel he will never describe to another living person), produces the following:

A theory of magical consciousness that explains what a Horcrux actually is (a mnemonic anchor, not a soul-fragment — the theorists were wrong about the metaphysics, right about the mechanics), how to reverse the process, and whether there is a way to achieve something like continuity of consciousness without fragmenting it.

The answer to the last question is: probably yes, but it requires a level of magical theory currently classified as theoretical and not yet empirically tested, and Harry intends to spend the next forty years testing it.

Tom Riddle — because he is now thinking of him by that name — is, by the time Harry is twenty, not quite a person again, but something that could theoretically become one, given care and time and conditions Harry is not certain can be provided.

He is not comfortable with this outcome. He has written thirty pages of notes about why he is not comfortable with it. He is doing it anyway because it is the closest to correct answer he has found, and he is the kind of person who does the closest to correct answer even when it makes him uncomfortable.

*THE END. (He is not sure if this is mercy or hubris. He has a bet on with Hermione. She thinks mercy. She is probably right.)*

### [ENDING-E] — THE ESCAPE
*Reachable from: Slytherin path + [N13-C] + [N17-THE-LIE] + [N18-B-ii]*

Harry runs.

He runs with the Stone in his pocket and the conversation in his head and the full knowledge that he has approximately four minutes before Quirrell recovers from the confusion Harry introduced into the situation by being faster and stranger than expected.

He runs into Dumbledore in the corridor.

He stops.

He hands Dumbledore the Stone.

He says: "There are multiple Horcruxes. I don't have the full list but I have a partial one. I'll give you everything I know. But I want something from you in return."

"What do you want?" Dumbledore asks. He is looking at Harry with the expression he sometimes gets, the one that Harry thinks means Dumbledore is surprised by something about Harry that he expected to understand and doesn't.

"The truth," Harry says. "Starting with what you actually know about my scar and why you have been managing my information diet for eleven months."

Dumbledore is quiet for a long moment.

"That," he says, "is a longer conversation than this corridor can hold." He looks at the Stone in his hands. "But yes. I think you have earned it."

They go to his office. The gargoyle steps aside.

Harry's notebook is in his pocket. He opens to a fresh page.

*THE END. (He is going to fill this page. And then the next one. That is what he does.)*

### [ENDING-F] — THE DARK COMPROMISE
*Reachable from: [N17-THE-PARTIAL] + [N18-B-i] (giving the Stone to Voldemort)*

Harry gives Voldemort the Stone.

In the moment of exchange — the Stone leaving his hand, entering Quirrell's — Harry has approximately six seconds in which he could take it back. He does not.

He tells himself: a present threat is better than an unknown one. He tells himself: he has the second half of the information now, the scar hypothesis confirmed, the knowledge of what he is carrying. He tells himself: Voldemort with a body is a problem that can be planned for.

He is not sure he believes all of this. But he has done it, and the time to have done otherwise has passed.

Voldemort, reforming, rebuilding — it takes months, it takes longer than expected — is a different threat than Voldemort the shadow. The world notices. The years that follow are not pleasant years. There are people who die who might not have died if Harry had held the Stone.

Harry knows this. He counts them. He keeps a list, not as punishment but as accounting.

He wins, eventually, in the last possible way that can be called winning. It takes until he is seventeen and it costs things he cannot get back.

He looks at the list in his notebook, fifteen names, and then at the confirmed list of Horcruxes on the page after it, all destroyed.

He adds one final note: *The methodology was sound. The information gained was necessary. The lives lost because of the information gap I chose to close via this method are real and are not abstractable. I will carry both of these facts simultaneously for the rest of my life.*

He closes the notebook.

*THE END. (There are no good endings available at this branch. There are honest ones. This is the most honest.)*

### [ENDING-G] — THE LOOP
*Reachable from: Ravenclaw path + [N08-A] (solo research) + having discovered the time-turner theoretical literature during the three months of preparation*

Harry does not go into the chamber the night he has planned.

He waits.

He waits because he has been doing a calculation for the past forty-eight hours that he cannot make come out clean, and when a calculation does not come out clean, the answer is usually that you are missing a variable and should go back and find it rather than act on incomplete information.

The variable he is missing is: *why did the protection specifically activate on the scar and nowhere else?*

He sits in the Ravenclaw library at two in the morning with a time-turner in his palm — he has borrowed it from McGonagall under a false pretext, which he feels guilty about — and the theoretical literature on time-loops open in front of him, and he thinks about what it would mean to go back. Not far. Six hours. Seven. To before Quirrell enters the chamber. To plant something that changes the configuration.

The time-turner can take him back six hours. If he uses it now and something goes wrong, he loops back again and starts from a slightly different point. He has, potentially, multiple attempts.

The problem with multiple attempts is you do not remember the failed ones.

Unless you write everything down before you turn the turner.

Harry stares at his notebook.

He starts writing very fast.

He writes everything he knows. Every hypothesis, every confirmed fact, every contingency. He writes the name of the Horcrux he believes is in his scar. He writes the locations of the two Horcruxes he has positively identified. He writes what he suspects Quirrell's timeline is.

He writes: *If you are reading this, you have been through at least one loop. Trust the notebook. Do not assume you know things you cannot verify from this page. Your memory is a lossy medium.*

He turns the time-turner.

The corridor blurs.

He arrives six hours earlier in an empty corridor, notebook in hand, and begins to plan.

*THE END — or the beginning, depending on how many loops it takes.*

*(He gets it in three. The third Harry, reading a notebook covered in two sets of handwriting, does something the first Harry could not: he acts on information he cannot remember gathering, because he built a system that does not rely on his memory. He trusts his past self. He wins. Then he sits in the aftermath and writes very careful notes about the phenomenology of retroactive knowledge, because this is exactly the kind of thing he needs to understand properly.)*
